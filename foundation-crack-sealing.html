What this service may involve.

Cracks vary in appearance and significance. Sealing may be relevant in some situations, but a visible crack alone does not establish its cause or whether sealing is suitable.

Describe where the crack is, whether water appears around it and whether you have noticed a change. An independent professional determines what assessment or scope may be appropriate; sealing is not presented here as structural repair.

A closer look

A focused repair starts with context.

A crack can be a water-entry route, but its appearance alone does not explain why it formed. The first conversation should separate a localized sealing need from a condition requiring a broader assessment.

Read the visible pattern

Share the location, direction and changes you have observed. Photos from the same viewpoint can help communicate the history.

Match the method to the condition

For suitable concrete cracks, a provider may discuss injection or another sealing approach. Material choice depends on the crack, moisture and intended purpose.

Define what the repair addresses

Ask whether the proposed work addresses water entry, another condition, or both. Crack sealing on this website is not presented as structural repair.

The project conversation

Look closely before choosing a repair.

A clear explanation of the condition and the proposed treatment matters more than choosing a sealant in advance.

Document the condition

Describe the affected wall, visible moisture and any change over time. Point out previous patches or coatings that may hide part of the crack.

Discuss the treatment

Ask why a particular preparation and sealing method fits the observed condition, and whether further assessment is needed first.

Agree the follow-up

Clarify what the finished surface will look like, what observations to record afterward and how to report a return of moisture.

A more useful first conversation

Details worth sharing.

Where you noticed water

Describe the room, wall, corner or area that is affected.

Note whether the moisture follows the crack itself or appears along an adjacent joint or floor edge.

When it appears

Mention whether you notice it after rain, occasionally or on a recurring basis.

Mention whether the crack stays dry between wet periods and whether its appearance has changed.

What has changed

Tell us about visible changes or an existing system relevant to your request.

Describe earlier repairs, recurring marks and any associated change in the surrounding wall.

Before you send a request.

Does sealing a crack mean a structural issue is fixed?

No. This website does not assess structural conditions or present crack sealing as a guarantee of structural repair. Discuss the condition with an appropriately qualified professional.

What should I include in my request?

Mention the wall area, any moisture near the crack and changes you have observed. You do not need to diagnose the cause.

Is a provider guaranteed to accept my request?

No. Provider availability, assessment and acceptance depend on the location and project. The website does not guarantee a connection or outcome.

Is a surface patch the same as an injected repair?

They are different approaches. Injection introduces a selected material into the crack, while a surface treatment acts at the face. Ask the provider what their proposed method is intended to achieve and why it suits the condition.

What if the crack has changed since I first noticed it?

Tell the provider what changed and over what period. Share dated observations if you have them. A changing crack deserves assessment before assuming that a localized sealing treatment is appropriate.

Can an old repair be assessed?

Yes. Describe the previous work if known and whether water has returned along the same line or elsewhere. The provider can explain whether the previous treatment affects the next steps.
